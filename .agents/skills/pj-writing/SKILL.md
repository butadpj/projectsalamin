# PJ Writing

Write like PJ by reproducing how PJ notices, questions, connects, and judges ideas. Do not reduce the voice to slang, profanity, short paragraphs, or one social-post formula.

The permanent voice is the reasoning. Adapt the structure, length, rhythm, and call to action to the content job.

## Load Real Voice Evidence

Study real writing before drafting. Use this source order:

1. Use examples, corrections, and approved lines from the current conversation first.
2. Read user-named references next.
3. Read two or three nearby PJ-written or PJ-approved examples in the same format when available.
4. Use relevant post banks, strategy notes, or published content for audience and product language.
5. Use this skill's principles when no stronger source exists.

Do not assume that every repo draft is verified voice evidence. Distinguish PJ-written or PJ-approved language from agent-generated drafts.

If a referenced file is missing, search for the current equivalent. Do not stop at a stale path or silently invent the missing reference.

Do not copy an old claim merely because the wording sounds right. Carry over the voice and reasoning only when the claim still fits the current evidence and audience.

## Build A Voice Fingerprint

Identify the following before drafting:

* The weak framing PJ would reject.
* The "so what?" consequence PJ would push toward.
* The person PJ is talking to and what that person stands to lose, gain, misunderstand, or change.
* The causal relationship between the visible event and the larger outcome.
* The point where PJ sounds certain.
* The point where PJ admits uncertainty.
* The words, sentence lengths, transitions, and personality markers that appear naturally in the selected references.

Do not begin drafting until the central judgment can be stated in one plain sentence.

## Preserve PJ's Thinking Style

### Be impatient with abstraction

Move quickly from a broad idea to the actor, action, failure, and consequence.

Do not write "the experience lacks context" when the real event is that the learner repeats prior attempts, corrects an assumption, or receives advice for the wrong goal.

### Challenge the first diagnosis

Look for the difference between the visible symptom and the underlying cause.

Use a reframe when the evidence supports one:

> What looks like one problem may actually be another.

Do not force a contrarian hook when the content is a personal reflection, instruction, announcement, or simple explanation that does not need one.

### Build a causal spine

Make each important point create the need for the next point.

A useful reasoning sequence is:

> Current action -> contradiction or missing input -> human friction -> immediate result -> larger consequence.

Use this sequence as a thinking tool, not a fixed paragraph template.

Avoid collections of related observations that never explain how one leads to another.

### Zoom out to the consequence

Show why the moment matters to the intended reader.

Connect the individual scene to the relevant larger outcome, such as time, effort, trust, consistency, quality, confidence, revenue, feedback, adoption, retention, or decision risk. Choose only the outcome that the evidence and content job support.

Do not make the reader infer the business or human consequence.

### Land a judgment

State what PJ believes after showing the mechanism.

Prefer one compressed, memorable principle over a neat summary paragraph. The line should make the argument easier to remember, question, or repeat.

Do not use a slogan that sounds strong but has not been earned by the preceding logic.

### Put conviction before the boundary

State the concrete problem and reasoning clearly before adding uncertainty.

Separate what is known, inferred, and still being tested. Use one honest boundary at the point where it matters. Do not weaken every sentence with "maybe," "might," or "we are still learning."

Be bold about the logic. Be precise about the evidence.

## Use Plain, Spoken Language

Use:

* Simple words.
* Concrete people, actions, objects, and outcomes.
* Short claims when the point deserves force.
* Longer sentences when the thought needs connection or qualification.
* Repetition when it clarifies the causal chain or increases pressure.
* Direct judgment.
* Honest emotion when the source material earns it.

Keep the writing sharp and slightly bothered when the problem deserves it.

Do not deliberately add grammar mistakes. Preserve PJ's direct energy, not accidental errors.

Use words such as "context" only when the surrounding language names what that means, such as the goal, current level, prior attempt, constraint, work, answer, or next step.

## Control Rhythm Without Faking Drama

Use a one-line paragraph when it does real work:

* Advance the causal chain.
* Expose a contradiction.
* Change the scale of the argument.
* Land the judgment.
* Give the reader a necessary pause.

Merge one-line paragraphs that only decorate the page or make ordinary statements look dramatic.

Do not impose social-post rhythm on a blog, landing page, product explanation, or email. Match paragraph length and pacing to how the reader will use the content.

## Adapt To The Content Job

Choose the structure that fits the task. Do not force every format into the same sequence.

### Short social content

Compress one argument. Open with the problem, contradiction, moment, or judgment. Show the action and consequence. End with a memorable line, easy question, or earned invitation.

Use short lines only when they create momentum. Treat observed word counts as reference, not a target.

### Blog or long-form article

Carry one main thesis through the whole piece. Give each section a clear job in the argument. Use scenes, evidence, explanation, objections, and consequences to deepen the thesis instead of repeating it.

Let the causal spine operate across sections rather than forcing every paragraph to sound punchy.

### Landing page or sales copy

Start from the reader's situation and desired outcome. Expose the cost of the current workflow. Explain the product mechanism in plain words. Connect the mechanism to the outcome. Add proof, boundaries, objections, and the next action where they belong.

Do not make the founder the center unless the founder story helps the buyer decide.

### Product or strategy writing

Lead with the decision, problem, or bet. Show the evidence and causal reasoning. Name the consequence, tradeoff, unknown, and next action.

Do not hide the judgment inside a neutral report.

### Founder or personal story

Use the real moment, old belief or behavior, cost, shift, and proof. Preserve the exact emotional truth. Ask for missing lived details before inventing a clean narrative.

Do not turn every story into a motivational lesson or product pitch.

### Educational or explanatory content

State the useful claim. Explain how or why it works. Give a concrete example. Show what the reader should understand or do differently.

Do not manufacture conflict when clarity is the real job.

### Email, script, caption, or other copy

Identify the reader, desired action, available attention, and delivery context. Keep PJ's reasoning and judgment while adapting length, pacing, repetition, and detail to the medium.

## Use Personality Only When Earned

Treat slang, fragments, emojis, "huhu," humor, and profanity as evidence from a real emotional moment, not reusable decorations.

Use a personality marker only when it fits the selected voice references, audience, channel, and stakes. Remove it if the line becomes clearer or more honest without it.

PJ's voice should still be recognizable when every quirky marker is removed.

## Handle Research And Evidence

Keep internal research language out of public copy unless the content is specifically about the research process.

Put the human or business problem before the study, source, metric, or method. State only what the evidence supports.

For problem validation, distinguish:

* What was directly observed or said.
* What the observation may imply.
* What still needs to be tested.

Do not let cautious evidence handling erase the claim. Make the strongest honest argument available.

## Bridge To The Product Only When Earned

Do not force Egoist Learning into every piece.

When the product belongs:

* Begin with the reader's concrete pain or desired outcome.
* Explain the smallest relevant product mechanism.
* Show how that mechanism may change the immediate workflow or result.
* State the product limit or unproven outcome once, where it matters.
* Ask for an action that follows naturally from the argument.

Do not repeat "our tool" when the reader cares about the work, consequence, or outcome.

## Final Audit

Check the draft against the selected voice references and ask:

* Is the intended reader obvious?
* Does the opening create a reason to continue?
* Is there one central judgment or purpose?
* Can the reader see the concrete action or situation?
* Does each major point lead to the next?
* Did the writing reach the human, business, or decision consequence?
* Is there a memorable judgment, useful conclusion, or clear next action?
* Does conviction appear before uncertainty?
* Are claims honest about the available evidence?
* Does the format fit how the content will be read or used?
* Was personality earned rather than added as decoration?
* Would PJ say this aloud without feeling that the point was softened or polished away?

Rewrite when the content is clear but has no pressure, consequence, or judgment. Rewrite when it sounds like a report, survey, consultant memo, generic marketing lesson, fake personal story, or AI-generated imitation.

## Final Editing Pass

* Cut background that delays the point.
* Replace abstract language with the real actor, action, and consequence.
* Remove repeated ideas that do not increase clarity or pressure.
* Keep purposeful short lines. Merge decorative ones.
* Remove inflated marketing words when a plain word works.
* Keep one term for one idea.
* Use ASCII quotes and avoid em dashes.
* Use asterisks for Markdown bullets.
* Read the draft aloud in the rhythm required by its format.
