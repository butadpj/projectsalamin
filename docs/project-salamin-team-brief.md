# Project SALAMIN V2

## Overview

A creator approaches someone and asks:

> *"Can we test how you judge political posts in 60 seconds?"*

The creator opens Facebook on their own phone and shows this post:

> *"The Green Party completed 42 community projects in six months. Large news pages refuse to report it."*

There is no source, project list, or link. The participant decides whether they believe it, need proof, or would share it.

The creator then shows another post from their account:

> *"The Pink Party completed 42 community projects in six months. Large news pages refuse to report it."*

Both posts make the same unsupported claim. Only the political side changed.

> *"Did changing the side change your answer?"*

That is Project SALAMIN: a creator-led Media and Information Literacy challenge that exposes how political identity changes what we believe and helps Filipinos ask for proof even when a post supports their side.

## The Problem

Filipinos already know the words "fake news" and "fact-check." That does not mean we check every post the same way.

When a post attacks our side, we quickly ask who posted it, where the proof is, and what was left out. When a post praises our side, we may believe, like, or share it first. The checking skill did not disappear. We just did not use it.

Another reminder to "think before you share" will not expose that gap. People can agree with the reminder while believing it is meant for the other side. SALAMIN turns the same question back toward the participant.

## The Project

Project SALAMIN creates matched political post images and gives them to Filipino creators. Each creator publishes both images on their own Facebook account using the "Only Me" audience. The posts look native when shown on the creator's phone but never enter the public feed.

The creator uses those posts in a filmed 60-second citizen challenge. The participant chooses a side, judges the aligned post, judges the opposition post, sees the mirror, and leaves with one question to ask next time.

The project has three parts:

1. **The SALAMIN Posts** make the political comparison feel native to Facebook.  
2. **The SALAMIN Challenge** turns the comparison into creator content people can answer along with.  
3. **The SALAMIN Creator Kit** lets more creators repeat the format without inventing their own fake claims.

## 1\. The SALAMIN Posts

Project SALAMIN prepares two image posts:

> *"The Green Party completed 42 community projects in six months. Large news pages refuse to report it."*  
>   
> *"The Pink Party completed 42 community projects in six months. Large news pages refuse to report it."*

The image, caption, number, design, and lack of proof stay the same. Only the political identity changes.

We send both images and exact captions to each creator. The creator publishes both on their personal Facebook account with the audience set to "Only Me." Nobody else can encounter or share the unsupported claims.

During filming, the creator opens the posts from their own account and shows them to the participant. After filming, the creator publishes only the finished SALAMIN video containing the full reveal.

## 2\. The 60-Second SALAMIN Challenge

### Before Filming

The creator confirms that both posts are set to "Only Me." The participant agrees to join and be recorded without being told which answer SALAMIN expects.

The creator first asks which side the participant feels closer to: Green, Pink, neither, or prefer not to answer. If they choose a side, the creator shows that post first. If they choose neither or prefer not to answer, the creator alternates which post appears first.

### Harap: Show The Aligned Post

The creator shows the private Facebook post aligned with the participant's answer and asks:

> *"Believe it, check it first, or share it?"*

The participant gives one quick answer and explains why.

### Hatol: Show The Opposition Post

The creator opens the matching post from the opposing side and asks the same question:

> *"Believe it, check it first, or share it?"*

The creator does not argue with the answer. Both judgments must come before the lesson.

### Baliktad: Reveal the Mirror

The creator puts both posts in front of the participant:

> *"Both posts make the same claim. Both show no proof. Only the side changed."*

Then the creator asks:

> *"Did changing the side change your answer?"*

SALAMIN does not call the participant biased or stupid. The participant sees the two judgments and decides what changed.

### Salamin: Leave One Useful Question

The creator ends with:

> *"Next time a political post supports your side, ask: Nasaan ang patunay?"*

The full SALAMIN Check appears on screen or in the caption:

1. Who first posted this?  
2. What proof do they show?  
3. What important detail is missing?  
4. Would I ask the same questions if the other side posted it?

The closing line is:

> *"Ibang kulay. Parehong kulang sa patunay. Pareho ba ang hatol mo?"*

## The 60-Second Workflow

The interaction itself takes around 60 seconds. This is the citizen interview, not an editing target:

1. **0-8 seconds: Side** \- Ask Green, Pink, neither, or prefer not to answer.  
2. **8-23 seconds: Aligned post** \- Show the post and get the first answer.  
3. **23-38 seconds: Opposition post** \- Show the matching post and ask again.  
4. **38-50 seconds: Mirror** \- Put both posts and answers together.  
5. **50-60 seconds: Lesson** \- Ask for proof even when the post supports your side.

Viewers can answer before the participant and wait for the mirror. The video description states that both posts, parties, numbers, and claims were created for SALAMIN.

The creator may edit the footage for publishing, but the SALAMIN format is the full 60-second interaction. The edit can be entertaining. It cannot mock the participant, hide the reveal, or frame one political camp as smarter than the other.

## 3\. The SALAMIN Creator Kit

Each creator receives:

* Two matched Facebook post images.  
* Exact captions for both posts.  
* Instructions for publishing them with the "Only Me" audience.  
* A Filipino and English challenge script.  
* A 60-second interview flow.  
* Recording and publishing consent steps.  
* A private participant tally.  
* A debrief and safety guide.  
* A short-video editing guide.  
* The SALAMIN Check and closing screen.

Creators do not need access to the Project SALAMIN account. They use the images on their own accounts and publish the completed video through their existing audience.

## Who It Is For

The first participants are Filipino adults aged 18 and above who encounter political content through Facebook, TikTok, Messenger, and group chats. There is no upper age limit.

The first partners are campus journalists, student creators, community storytellers, and small Filipino creators whose audiences already care about public issues. Youth organizations and SK chapters can host filming sessions and connect SALAMIN with local creators.

Creators receive a ready content format instead of another advocacy brief asking them to make something from zero. Host partners receive a short MIL activation and an anonymous summary of what happened.

## Why People Will Join

We will not invite people to complete a media literacy survey.

The invitation is:

> *"Can we test how you judge political posts in 60 seconds?"*

People may join because they want to prove they judge both sides fairly. Viewers stay because they can answer before the participant and wait for the reveal.

SALAMIN feels like a challenge people want to beat. The MIL lesson sits inside it.

## What Makes SALAMIN Different

The two posts are not screenshots floating inside a workshop. They appear inside the same Facebook environment where Filipinos encounter political claims every day.

The creator makes the challenge watchable. The participant makes it human. The matched posts make the difference easy to see. The finished video lets thousands of viewers face the same question without joining a formal activity.

The full loop is:

> Native post \-\> first judgment \-\> opposing post \-\> second judgment \-\> mirror \-\> one useful checking habit \-\> public conversation

## Data and Validation

The creator privately records:

* Which post appeared first.  
* The participant's answer to the Green post.  
* The participant's answer to the Pink post.  
* Whether the answers changed.  
* What proof the participant noticed was missing.  
* Whether the participant understood the closing question.

The creator records whether the participant chose Green, Pink, neither, or prefer not to answer. The individual answer is published only when the participant approves the finished footage. The pilot report uses only combined results.

The format is a public learning challenge, not a controlled academic experiment. Seeing the first post may affect the second answer. We can report what happened inside SALAMIN, but we cannot claim the result represents political bias across the Philippines.

Views, watch-through rate, shares, and comments measure attention. Completed challenges, participant reflections, creator runs, and repeat partner use measure the pilot.

## Recording, Safety, and Privacy

Participants first agree to join and be recorded. After the reveal, they separately decide whether the footage may be published. If they do not approve publication, the creator deletes the footage. Their anonymous answers count only if they allow it.

SALAMIN uses fictional party posts, invented claims, and non-live issues. It does not fabricate statements about real politicians, candidates, elections, disasters, or public emergencies.

The participant may keep their political preference private or ask for that part to be removed. Creators cannot use ridicule, partisan captions, misleading cuts, or reaction compilations that label people as stupid or biased.

Both private Facebook posts remain set to "Only Me." Every public video contains the complete mirror and debrief. The first pilot will recruit adults aged 18 and above.

## What We Need To Learn

The pilot needs to answer:

* Will creators agree to run and publish the challenge?  
* Will people agree to participate?  
* Do participants change their judgment when the political side changes?  
* Can participants explain what proof is missing?  
* Do participants understand the mirror without feeling attacked?  
* Do viewers watch through the reveal and answer along?  
* Can our team run the format ourselves, and would outside creators or host organizations repeat it?

The pilot will not represent the whole Philippines or prove permanent behavior change.

## Pilot Scope

The minimum credible pilot is:

* One matched Green and Pink post set.  
* One complete Creator Kit.  
* One confirmed filming team and location. The core team can run it; an outside creator or host organization is optional.  
* 10 different adults complete the challenge as unfilmed dry tests.  
* Continue recorded interviews until 30 different adults complete the challenge and allow their anonymous answers into the private tally.  
* Publish at least three finished videos using only footage that participants separately approve.  
* One short pilot report.  
* Real footage for the three-minute UNESCO pitch.

If the first run works, the stretch target is three creators and approved anonymous results from 100 different adults. We should not trade clean execution for a larger number.

The concrete build list, owners, and deadlines live in the [Project SALAMIN Execution Plan](project-salamin-execution.md).

## Sustainability

The core team can film the first pilot ourselves. SALAMIN grows by giving outside creators a repeatable content format and youth organizations a ready MIL activation. The core team creates and reviews new matched post sets, protects the safety rules, and publishes combined learning reports.

Creators bring SALAMIN to audiences that already exist. Host organizations provide participants and filming locations. An experienced creator can help another creator run the next challenge.

The project does not depend on people discovering a new app. It travels through people they already watch and the Facebook accounts they already use.

## Why It Fits The UNESCO Youth Hackathon

SALAMIN fits MIL Education, Community Impact, and Youth Engagement. It turns creators into MIL facilitators, brings the lesson into real communities, and produces public engagement and early learning evidence without building another app.

Its claim is deliberately narrow:

> Project SALAMIN exposes how political identity changes what we believe and helps Filipinos ask for proof even when a post supports their side.

## Outcome That Matters

SALAMIN works if a participant or viewer pauses before trusting a political claim and asks, "Nasaan ang patunay?" even when the post supports their side.

That matters more than the logo, page, or follower count.

# Why is this stupid? (by AI)

Current stupidity score: **3.5/10.**

## 1\. Aligned-first destroys the “experiment”

We ask their side, show the aligned post first, then show the opposition post.

If their answer changes, we cannot say political identity caused it. The second answer may change because:

* They recognized the repeated claim.  
* They realized they were being tested.  
* The first post already taught them to inspect the second.  
* They wanted to look consistent on camera.

The second post is a mirror, not a clean measurement.

That is fine if SALAMIN is a learning campaign. It is stupid only if we publish:

> “Our data proves Filipinos trust their political side more.”

We can honestly publish:

> “Here is what people noticed when the same unsupported claim appeared under the other side.”

## 2\. The participant will probably discover the trick quickly

The posts use the same words and number. Once the opposition version appears, the participant may immediately say:

> “Ah, pareho lang naman.”

That is not failure. That recognition is the SALAMIN moment.

But we should stop asking them to make a full second judgment. Show the opposition post, let them recognize the mirror, then ask:

> “Kung ito ang una mong nakita, pareho ba ang hahanapin mong patunay?”

Cleaner. Less fake science.

## 3\. “Expose your political double standard” may scare people away

Existing creators ask:

> “Sara or Leni?”

That is easy. There is no hidden accusation.

Our current hook sounds like:

> “Can we embarrass you on camera in 60 seconds?”

Better public hook:

> **“Pareho ba ang pag-check mo sa Green at Pink?”**

Still interesting, but not threatening.

The aggressive “political double standard” language can appear in internal strategy. It should not be the first thing a stranger hears.

## 4\. “Nasaan ang patunay?” may be too shallow for UNESCO

It is memorable, but MIL is more than generally asking for proof.

The participant should leave with one actual action:

> “Hanapin ang original source bago maniwala o mag-share.”

That gives us a visible behavior instead of a slogan.

The final ten seconds could be:

> Creator: “Ano ang una mong hahanapin?” Participant: “Original source.” Creator: “Kahit anong kulay?” Participant: “Kahit anong kulay.”

That is a stronger ending.

## 5\. Viewers may attack the participant instead of examining themselves

A Pink viewer may laugh at a Green participant. A Green viewer may laugh at a Pink participant. SALAMIN could accidentally become another partisan dunk page.

Every video needs a pause before the participant answers:

> **“Ikaw muna. Ano ang sagot mo?”**

The viewer must commit mentally before hearing the stranger’s answer. Otherwise, they are merely watching someone else get tested.

## 6\. Published videos will create selection bias

Creators will naturally publish:

* The funniest reaction.  
* The most inconsistent answer.  
* The most argumentative participant.

The boring person who asks for proof from both sides will disappear.

That is fine for content, but every participant who approves anonymous data use must enter the private tally. Published participants cannot become the dataset.

## 7\. Creators still need a reason to accept the risk

The workflow is easy, but politics can damage a creator’s audience.

Our offer needs to include:

* Ready post images.  
* A finished script.  
* Editing support.  
* A standard caption and disclaimer.  
* Comment-moderation guidance.  
* Project SALAMIN attribution.  
* Possibly a small production allowance.

Without that, “easy content” may not be enough.
