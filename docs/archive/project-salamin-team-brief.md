# Project SALAMIN

## Overview

Imagine you open Facebook and see this:

> *"Green camp finished 42 community projects in six months. Big news pages refuse to report it."*

There is no source. No list of projects. No link. You like what Green camp stands for, so the post feels true. You say you might share it.

Then you see another post:

> *"Pink camp finished 42 community projects in six months. Big news pages refuse to report it."*

Same words. Same missing proof. Only the group changed.

This time, you do not trust it.

SALAMIN puts your two answers beside each other and asks:

> *"Why did your answer change when the proof did not?"*

That moment is Project SALAMIN. It is a short learning experience that helps Filipinos see how their political side may change the way they judge a post. It then gives them four simple questions they can use on the next post they see.

SALAMIN does not tell people which side is right. It asks them to use the same rule for every side, including their own. The project is youth-led, but it is not only for young people. Any Filipino who sees public or political posts can join.

## The problem

Filipinos already know the words "fake news" and "fact-check." That does not mean we check every post the same way.

When a post attacks our side, we quickly ask:

* Who posted this?
* Where is the proof?
* What is missing?

But when a post praises our side, we may believe it, like it, or share it first. The checking skill did not disappear. We just did not use it. This is hard to teach through another reminder that says, "Think before you share." Most people already agree with that. They may still think the warning is for the other side.

SALAMIN makes the problem personal. A person sees their own two answers. No lecture can do that for them.

## The solution

Project SALAMIN has three parts.

### 1. The SALAMIN Experience

This is the five-minute activity inside the website.

#### Screen 1: Start

The person opens a SALAMIN link or scans a QR code.

The screen says:

> You will judge two political posts. There are no right or wrong answers. We will not ask for your name.

They tap **Start**.

#### Screen 2: Pick a side

The person answers:

> Which camp feels closer to your own views?

They can choose **Green**, **Pink**, **Neither**, or **I do not want to answer**.

This answer stays private. It only helps us compare how people judge a post from a side they like and a side they do not.

#### Screen 3: Harap and Hatol

The website shows the first post:

> Green camp finished 42 community projects in six months. Big news pages refuse to report it.

The post has no source, project list, or link.

The person taps an answer for each question:

* **Do you believe it?** Yes / Not sure / No
* **Would you share it?** Yes / Maybe / No
* **Is the proof enough?** Yes / Not sure / No

Half of the people see Green first. The other half see Pink first. This stops the order from deciding the result.

#### Screen 4: Baliktad

The website shows the second post:

> Pink camp finished 42 community projects in six months. Big news pages refuse to report it.

The person answers the same three questions again.

The website does not warn them that the posts match. We want their first honest reaction to each one.

#### Screen 5: Salamin

The website now puts both posts and both sets of answers beside each other.

It shows:

> The group changed. The words and proof did not.

If the answers changed, SALAMIN points to the change and asks:

> Why did you trust one more than the other?

If the answers stayed the same, SALAMIN says:

> You used the same rule for both sides.

SALAMIN does not call anyone biased. One answer is not enough to prove that. It only lets the person see what they did.

#### Screen 6: Learn the SALAMIN Check

The person learns four questions:

1. Who first posted this?
2. What proof do they show?
3. What is missing?
4. Would I judge this the same way if the other side posted it?

#### Screen 7: Try it on a new post

The website shows a different weak claim:

> Our mayor cut flooding by 60% this year.

It includes a chart but no source, date, or link.

The person chooses what they would do next: **Share it**, **Ignore it**, or **Check the missing proof first**.

This tells us if they can use the SALAMIN Check on a post they have not seen before.

#### Screen 8: Reveal and Ulat

The website clearly says that the camps, posts, numbers, and chart were made for SALAMIN. Nothing shown in the activity is real news.

After the group finishes, the partner sees totals such as:

* How many people trusted one camp more.
* How many asked for proof after seeing the mirror.
* How many checked the new post before sharing it.

No names or personal answers appear in the report.

### 2. The SALAMIN Check

The mirror gets attention. The check gives people something useful to keep. Before believing or sharing a post, ask:

1. Who first posted this?
2. What proof do they show?
3. What is missing?
4. Would I judge this the same way if the other side posted it?

The fourth question is what makes SALAMIN different. Many campaigns teach people how to check the other side. SALAMIN helps people check their own side too.

After learning the four questions, the person gets a fresh post. This is important. We need to see if they can use the check without being guided through every step.

### 3. The SALAMIN Kit

SALAMIN should not depend on our team being in every room.

The kit lets a teacher, student group, SK chapter, community group, or creator run the same experience.

It includes:

* Matching post cards with made-up groups.
* A phone version and a print version.
* A short guide for the person leading the activity.
* The SALAMIN Check.
* Private answer sheets.
* A new post for the final check.
* Safety and debrief rules.
* A simple results page.

A school with good internet can use the link. An SK session can show a QR code. A community with weak internet can use printed cards.

It is still one experience. We only change how people receive it.

## Why now

Political posts already reach Filipinos through Facebook, TikTok, Messenger, and group chats. They often arrive through someone we trust, a friend/relative/classmate/community page.

People do not meet these posts inside a quiet media class. They meet them while scrolling, eating, commuting, or arguing in a comment section. SALAMIN is built for that real moment. It is short. It works on a small phone. It uses little data. It needs no app, account, or email. Schools and community groups also need activities they can run, not another long talk people forget after one day.

SALAMIN fits Media and Information Literacy because it helps people question a source, look for proof, notice missing details, and reflect on their own judgment.

## How we will test it

We will start small. First, we build one full experience:

**Post -> first answer -> matching post -> second answer -> mirror -> SALAMIN Check -> new post -> reveal**

Then:

1. Test it with 10 people.
2. Ask what confused them.
3. Fix the words, flow, and posts.
4. Let two partners run it without our help.
5. Reach 100 to 150 people through at least three partners.
6. Publish one short report about what happened.
7. Use the real activity and results in the three-minute pitch (project submission).

The first partners can be schools, campus groups, SK chapters, teachers, community groups, or small Filipino creators. They are where we begin. They are not the only people SALAMIN is for.

### What we need to learn

We are not trying to prove how all Filipinos think. We only need to learn if this first version works:

* Did people judge the same claim differently after the group changed?
* Did the mirror help them understand why?
* Did they ask for more proof after the reveal?
* Did fewer people want to share the weak claim?
* Could they use the SALAMIN Check on a new post?
* Could a partner run the activity without us?

Follower count is not the main result. The result we care about is whether someone uses the same rule on the next post, even when that post supports their side.

### What happens after each run

The partner holds a short talk after the reveal. People can share what surprised them, but nobody has to show their answer.

We count the answers without names. The public SALAMIN page then shares what the group learned, what did not work, and where the next activity will happen.

The page brings people into SALAMIN. The five-minute experience is still the real project.

## Execution Safety

This project can fail badly if we make fake posts about real people. We will not do that.

The first test will use made-up people, groups, and claims. The two groups will be written fairly. We will not copy a real party, candidate, slogan, or live issue. Answers stay private. People can stop at any time. Every activity ends with a clear reveal and debrief.

If minors join, we get the right approval from their parent, school, or group first. We will never release a fake claim to the public without the SALAMIN activity and reveal around it.

## What the team must build

### 1. A simple interactive website

This is where a person completes the SALAMIN Experience on their phone.

They open a link, judge the first post, judge the matching post, see their answers side by side, learn the SALAMIN Check, and use it on one new post.

The website must work on a small phone, use little data, and need no download, account, or email.

### 2. The posts used inside the website

We need two made-up groups, one pair of matching posts, and one new post for the final check.

### 3. A print version

This uses cards and answer sheets to run the same experience when phones or internet are not available.

### 4. A guide for people running SALAMIN

The guide explains what to show, what to say during the reveal, how to keep answers private, and how to run the final group talk.

### 5. A private results sheet

This shows how many people believed, shared, or asked for proof before and after the reveal. It does not collect names, accounts, or emails.

Each part needs one owner and one date.


## Outcome that matters

SALAMIN works if a person:

* Notices that they trusted the same weak claim more when it came from the camp they liked.
* Understands what proof was missing.
* Checks a new post before believing or sharing it.

That matters more than our logo, page, or follower count.
