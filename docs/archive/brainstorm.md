# Project SALAMIN

## The Problem

The Philippines is divided into political camps. DDS supporters can quickly see what is wrong with a Kakampink post. Kakampinks can quickly see what is wrong with a DDS post. But give people the same weak claim from their own side and the standard can change.

Imagine a Facebook post saying, "Our leader completed 100 projects in one year. Mainstream media refuses to report it." Put Duterte's name and image on it. Then put Leni's name and image on the same post. Same claim. Same missing source. Different reaction.

We are not saying every DDS or Kakampink behaves this way. We want to test whether political identity changes how much evidence people demand. Knowing how to fact-check is not enough if we only use that skill against the other side.

## The Idea

Project SALAMIN is a youth-led Media and Information Literacy intervention. It lets Filipino youth experience their own political bias, teaches them a simple way to check it, then gives youth organizations a kit for running the same experience in their communities.

The social media brand is not the whole project. It recruits participants, distributes experiments, publishes results, and documents community runs. The actual project has three parts: the SALAMIN Experience, the SALAMIN Check, and the SALAMIN Kit.

## 1. The SALAMIN Experience

Participants see a post, video, screenshot, or statistic and decide whether they believe or would share it. We then reveal the source, missing context, edit, or mirrored version. They see whether changing the political identity changed their judgment even when the evidence stayed the same.

The experience follows five steps:

1. **Harap** - Show the information.
2. **Hatol** - Ask participants to judge or share it.
3. **Baliktad** - Reveal the missing context or mirrored version.
4. **Salamin** - Let them compare the evidence with their first judgment.
5. **Ulat** - Publish the result and what we learned.

The point is not to tell people which political side to choose. The point is to make them notice when their side changes how they judge information.

## 2. The SALAMIN Check

The reveal is only the wake-up call. Participants leave with four questions they can use on the next post:

* Who is the original source?
* What evidence supports the claim?
* What context is missing?
* Would I judge this the same way if the other side posted it?

This is the MIL skill we want to build: use the same standard of evidence for every side, including your own.

## 3. The SALAMIN Kit

The kit allows student councils, campus publications, SK chapters, youth organizations, and educators to run SALAMIN without us standing in the room.

It includes:

* Ready-to-run fictional claim cards and mirrored versions.
* A facilitator guide for the reveal and discussion.
* Participant consent, debrief, and safety rules.
* Before-and-after questions and one transfer test.
* A results template for publishing a local SALAMIN report.

Project SALAMIN maintains the kit, trains partner facilitators, and turns results from different communities into public learning.

## Who It Is For

The first audience is Filipino youth aged 18-30 who regularly encounter political content online. We will begin with campus and community youth groups because they already have participants, facilitators, and spaces where the reveal can become a real conversation.

The wider beneficiaries are youth organizations that need an MIL activity they can run, adapt, and measure without building an app or inviting a political lecturer.

## What Makes It Different

Most MIL campaigns tell people to verify sources, check context, and think before sharing. The advice is correct, but people can agree with it while believing that only the other side needs it.

SALAMIN makes the participant catch the inconsistency in their own judgment first. Then it teaches the checking method. We are not testing whether people can repeat an MIL lesson. We are testing whether they can apply the same lesson when political identity gets involved.

## Boundaries

Public experiments will use fictional people, groups, and non-live issues. We will not publish fabricated claims about real candidates or trick the general public without a clear and immediate debrief. We will collect only the minimum anonymous data needed for the pilot.

These limits are part of the project. Without them, SALAMIN could create the same harm it claims to study and would conflict with UNESCO's values of peace, diversity, and freedom of expression.

## What We Need To Prove

Follower count is attention. It is not impact. We need to know:

* Did participants judge identical claims differently when the political identity changed?
* After the reveal, did they demand stronger evidence?
* Could they use the SALAMIN Check on a new post they had not seen before?
* Did willingness to share an unsupported claim decrease?
* Could a partner organization run the experience using the kit?
* Would participants and partners recommend running it again?

The pilot is exploratory. It will not represent the whole Philippines. Its job is to show that the experience works, the learning can transfer, and the format can be repeated.

## Distribution

We should borrow communities before trying to grow a large audience from zero. Start with student councils, campus publications, youth organizations, SK chapters, educators, and small Filipino creators.

Use Facebook for recruitment and community distribution. Use TikTok for short reveals and participant reflections. Use physical sessions for facilitated discussion and footage. The strongest content will come from real SALAMIN runs, not generic educational posts.

## Working Target Before August 16

* Build one complete SALAMIN Experience and facilitator kit.
* Test it with 10 people and fix what they misunderstand.
* Run at least two partner-led sessions.
* Reach 100-150 completed participants.
* Work with at least three youth or community partners.
* Publish one short SALAMIN pilot report.
* Produce a three-minute pitch using real footage and results.

These are targets. We have not earned the numbers yet.

## Why It Fits The UNESCO Hackathon

SALAMIN fits MIL Education, Community Impact, and Youth Engagement. It is a creative learning experience, a community intervention, and a reusable youth organization campaign.

Its value is not another page telling Filipinos to stop believing fake news. It gives young people a safe way to experience political bias, a method for checking it, and a kit for helping other communities do the same.

## Immediate Next Step

Do not launch a full content campaign yet. Build one fictional mirrored claim, the four-question SALAMIN Check, the reveal, the transfer test, and the facilitator guide. Test the complete experience with 10 people.

The first useful milestone is not launching the page. It is proving that someone can catch their own bias and judge the next claim more carefully.
